Engineering Rigor, Data Limitations & Assumptions
1. Redundant Columns & Noise Removal

In the raw data, living room sensor columns T2 and RH_2 are strictly identical to outdoor weather readings T_out and RH_out throughout the 19,735 rows. Keeping them would cause severe multicollinearity. Furthermore, rv1 and rv2 were synthetic random uniform variables created during the original UCI benchmark; they were dropped to avoid overfitting to noise.

2. Resampling Rationale (1-min vs. 10-min)

A naive model on raw 1-minute data yields weak predictive signal (R² ~0.44, MAE ~26.4 Wh) due to stochastic 60-second appliance switching transients. Resampling to 10-minute mean aggregation filters high-frequency jitter into physical power trends, boosting R² to 0.67 and reducing MAE to 9.58 Wh without loss of actionable dispatch utility.

3. Temporal Scope Limitation

The dataset spans exactly 13.7 days (2016-01-11 to 2016-01-24) during winter in Stambruges, Belgium. Consequently, the model cannot extrapolate to summer air conditioning behavior or long-term seasonal oscillations. In production, this requires an incremental retrainer or continuous fine-tuning pipeline.

4. Absence of Sub-Metered Appliance Channels

Whole-home appliance load is captured as a lump sum without per-circuit energy disaggregation (NILM). Recommendations are therefore calculated counterfactually through scenario re-prediction with unit Wh tracking, rather than sub-circuit switching.
